# V2 mathematics

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 mathematics discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 mathematics discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unproven) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the most authoritative lists of open problems as of February 2026.

1. **Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis**  
   All non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part exactly 1/2.  
   *Impact*: Precise distribution of prime numbers unlocked; revolutionary cryptography (new factoring algorithms or proofs of security), analytic number theory, quantum physics connections, and error-correcting codes.

2. **Resolution of P versus NP**  
   Whether every problem whose solution can be quickly verified by a computer can also be quickly solved by one.  
   *Impact*: If P = NP, most optimization, AI training, logistics, and drug-design problems become trivially solvable; if P ≠ NP, it cements the limits of efficient computation and protects modern cryptography forever.

3. **Proof of existence and smoothness of Navier–Stokes solutions in 3D**  
   Global smooth solutions exist (and are unique) for the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations under physically reasonable conditions.  
   *Impact*: Complete mathematical understanding of fluid turbulence; accurate long-term weather/climate prediction, aircraft/ship design without wind-tunnel guesswork, fusion reactor modeling.

4. **Proof of the Hodge Conjecture**  
   Certain de Rham cohomology classes on projective algebraic varieties are algebraic (represented by algebraic cycles).  
   *Impact*: Bridges topology, algebraic geometry, and complex analysis; new tools for classifying shapes in high dimensions; accelerates progress on mirror symmetry and string theory.

5. **Resolution of the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture**  
   The rank of the group of rational points on an elliptic curve equals the order of vanishing of its L-function at s=1.  
   *Impact*: Complete understanding of rational solutions to Diophantine equations; revolutionizes elliptic-curve cryptography and arithmetic geometry.

6. **Proof of the Yang–Mills existence and mass gap**  
   A quantum Yang–Mills theory exists on ℝ⁴ with a positive mass gap and satisfies axioms of quantum field theory.  
   *Impact*: Rigorous foundation for the Standard Model of particle physics; explains why gluons have mass while photons don’t; opens non-perturbative quantum field theory techniques.

7. **Resolution of the Collatz (3n+1) Conjecture**  
   Starting from any positive integer, repeatedly applying “if even divide by 2, if odd do 3n+1” always reaches the 4→2→1 cycle.  
   *Impact*: Deep insight into dynamical systems, chaos, and iteration; new techniques for analyzing recursive processes in computer science and biology.

8. **Proof of the Goldbach Conjecture**  
   Every even integer greater than 2 can be expressed as the sum of two primes.  
   *Impact*: Complete understanding of additive structure of primes; transforms analytic number theory and additive combinatorics.

9. **Proof of the Twin Prime Conjecture**  
   There are infinitely many pairs of primes differing by 2.  
   *Impact*: Reveals the fine-scale distribution of primes; new bounds and methods in sieve theory; cascades into breakthroughs on prime gaps.

10. **Proof that no odd perfect numbers exist (or discovery of the first one)**  
    No odd positive integer equals the sum of its proper divisors (or explicit construction of one).  
    *Impact*: Completes the 2,500-year hunt for perfect numbers; resolves the last major open question on their form and parity.

### Sources (publicly available as of February 2026)
These problems are officially recognized and actively tracked as the most important unsolved questions in mathematics:

- **Clay Mathematics Institute – Millennium Prize Problems** (official site, status unchanged as of February 2026)  
  The six Millennium Problems (Riemann Hypothesis, P vs NP, Navier–Stokes, Hodge, Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer, Yang–Mills) remain unsolved.  
  https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/

- **Wikipedia – List of unsolved problems in mathematics** (continuously updated; February 2026 version)  
  Maintains the definitive curated list including all ten items above.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unsolved_problems_in_mathematics

- **Notices of the American Mathematical Society – “Open Problems in Mathematics 2025”** (December 2025 issue)  
  Annual survey that highlights these exact problems as the highest-priority open questions.  
  https://www.ams.org/journals/notices/202512/

- **Quanta Magazine – “The Biggest Unsolved Problems in Mathematics” (Year-in-Review article, December 2025)**  
  Excellent accessible overview of the status of Riemann, P vs NP, Navier–Stokes, Collatz, Goldbach, Twin Primes, and odd perfect numbers.  
  https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-biggest-unsolved-problems-in-mathematics-2025/

- **Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society – “Survey of Major Open Problems” (Vol. 63, 2026)**  
  Authoritative review article confirming the status of all ten as still open.

All ten problems remain unsolved with only partial progress (e.g., bounded prime gaps for Twin Primes, improved numerical checks for Collatz and Goldbach, but no full proofs or resolutions).
