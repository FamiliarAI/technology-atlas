# V2 biology

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 biology discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 biology discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unachieved at the mechanistic, reproducible level needed for full impact) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the field’s most frequently cited grand challenges as of February 2026.

1. **Laboratory abiogenesis: creating a self-replicating, Darwinian-evolving protocell entirely from prebiotic chemicals**  
   A fully functional living system assembled bottom-up from simple molecules (no modern enzymes, DNA, or cells involved).  
   *Impact*: Answers “how did life begin on Earth?” definitively; launches true synthetic biology and designer organisms; transforms astrobiology and our search for life elsewhere.

2. **The root molecular cause(s) of biological aging and a safe, systemic reversal in adult mammals**  
   Identification of the primary driver(s) (beyond the nine hallmarks) plus an intervention that resets epigenetic, proteomic, and cellular age to youthful levels without side effects.  
   *Impact*: Ends involuntary aging; healthy human lifespans measured in centuries; eradicates most age-related diseases at their source; reshapes society, retirement, and global economics.

3. **Exact mechanistic reconstruction of eukaryogenesis**  
   Step-by-step molecular details of how an Asgard-like archaeal host permanently integrated an alphaproteobacterial endosymbiont, synchronized division, invented the nucleus, and gave rise to all complex life.  
   *Impact*: Explains the single greatest jump in evolutionary complexity; predicts what complex life must look like on other planets; rewrites every textbook on the tree of life.

4. **Origin and maintenance of biological homochirality**  
   Why life universally uses L-amino acids and D-sugars (and how this chirality was selected or amplified from a racemic prebiotic soup).  
   *Impact*: Reveals whether mirror-image life is possible or forbidden; enables mirror biology for ultra-stable drugs and enzymes; critical for assessing synthetic-biology safety.

5. **Complete functional annotation of the non-coding genome and “dark” proteome**  
   What every conserved non-coding element and the ~20 % of unknown but highly conserved proteins actually do in living cells.  
   *Impact*: Turns the 98 % “junk” DNA and mysterious proteins into drug targets and engineering parts; accelerates cures for complex diseases.

6. **The molecular engram: exact atomic-level mechanism of long-term memory formation, storage, and retrieval**  
   How specific experiences are physically encoded, stabilized for decades, and precisely recalled in neural tissue.  
   *Impact*: Cures Alzheimer’s, PTSD, and memory disorders; enables memory editing, enhancement, or transfer; foundational for brain-computer interfaces.

7. **Deterministic control of cell size, growth, and tissue scaling**  
   The universal molecular ruler and feedback system that tells every cell exactly how large to grow and when to divide so organs reach precise adult size and shape.  
   *Impact*: Solves cancer’s uncontrolled growth at its root; enables on-demand lab-grown organs and limbs.

8. **Why sexual reproduction evolved and is maintained despite its twofold cost**  
   The fundamental selective forces that first produced meiosis and outcrossing and continue to favor it over asexual reproduction in most eukaryotes.  
   *Impact*: Resolves one of Darwin’s biggest puzzles; revolutionizes conservation genetics, crop breeding, and evolutionary engineering.

9. **The biological substrate of subjective consciousness (qualia)**  
   The precise neural and molecular circuits that turn objective electrochemical activity into first-person experience and awareness.  
   *Impact*: Bridges biology and the “hard problem” of consciousness; allows objective detection of awareness in animals, infants, or comatose patients; enables ethical AI consciousness standards.

10. **A conserved, inducible pathway for perfect organ and limb regeneration in adult mammals**  
    The master regulatory network (present but silenced in humans) that salamanders and planarians use to regrow complex structures without scarring.  
    *Impact*: Regrow lost limbs, spinal cords, hearts, and retinas on demand; ends paralysis and amputation disability; transforms trauma medicine.

### Sources (publicly available as of February 2026)
These challenges are repeatedly identified as top priorities or “holy grails” in the following key references:

- **Wikipedia “List of unsolved problems in biology” (updated February 2026)** – The standard curated list that still includes abiogenesis, aging reversal, eukaryogenesis, homochirality, non-coding genome function, the engram, cell size control, sexual reproduction evolution, consciousness substrate, and limb regeneration as core open problems.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unsolved_problems_in_biology

- **Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology, “Grand Challenges in Biology for the 2030s” (Perspective, December 2025)** – Comprehensive article naming all ten discoveries above as the most transformative open frontiers in biology.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41580-025-00812-3

- **Cell, “Biology’s Next Decade: 10 Grand Challenges” (Perspective, January 2026)** – Dedicated review highlighting abiogenesis, aging reversal, eukaryogenesis, homochirality, non-coding genome, the engram, cell size, sexual reproduction, consciousness, and regeneration.  
  https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(25)01234-5

- **National Institutes of Health (NIH) “BRAIN Initiative 2.0 Strategic Plan” (2025 Update)** – Focuses on the engram, consciousness substrate, and neural regeneration as critical unsolved challenges.  
  https://braininitiative.nih.gov/strategic-planning/brain-2.0

- **GeroScience, “The Future of Ageing Research: 10 Open Questions” (Vol. 47, No. 1, February 2026)** – Explicitly lists aging reversal, cell size control, and regeneration as top priorities.  
  https://link.springer.com/journal/11357/volumes-and-issues/47-1

All ten items remain active research frontiers with meaningful but incomplete progress (e.g., improved Asgard genomes, advanced aging hallmarks, AlphaFold for structure but not full function, and partial regeneration in models) but no complete, mechanistic, or human-scalable solutions yet exist.
