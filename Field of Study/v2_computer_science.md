# V2 computer science

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 computer science discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 computer science discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unachieved at the scalable, practical level needed for full impact) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the field’s most frequently cited grand challenges as of February 2026.

1. **Resolution of P versus NP**  
   A definitive proof that P = NP or P ≠ NP, ideally with constructive techniques or tight barriers.  
   *Impact*: If P = NP, most optimization, scheduling, drug design, and logistics problems become efficiently solvable; if P ≠ NP, it cements the limits of efficient computation and secures modern cryptography forever.

2. **Explicit construction of one-way functions from minimal/standard assumptions**  
   Concrete, efficiently computable functions that are easy to evaluate but provably hard to invert (without relying on unproven conjectures like factoring).  
   *Impact*: Places all of public-key cryptography on rigorous foundations; enables provably secure protocols for everything from blockchain to cloud computing.

3. **Practical, efficient fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) with polylog overhead**  
   A scheme allowing arbitrary computation on encrypted data with only polylogarithmic slowdown, usable on real-world hardware.  
   *Impact*: Enables truly private cloud computing, secure machine-learning-as-a-service, and encrypted databases that remain searchable.

4. **Scalable fault-tolerant universal quantum computer (millions of logical qubits at low error rates)**  
   A system achieving full error-corrected logical qubits at scale, with gate fidelities and coherence times sufficient for useful algorithms beyond simulation.  
   *Impact*: Breaks current public-key crypto, simulates molecules for drug/materials discovery, optimizes global logistics, and solves certain NP-hard problems exponentially faster.

5. **Optimal matrix multiplication exponent ω = 2**  
   A deterministic algorithm multiplying two n × n matrices in O(n²) time (or arbitrarily close).  
   *Impact*: Transforms scientific computing, graphics, machine learning, and all linear-algebra-heavy applications; speeds up everything from weather models to neural-net training by orders of magnitude.

6. **Resolution of the Unique Games Conjecture**  
   Proof or disproof that the Unique Games problem is as hard as the worst-case approximation for many NP-hard problems.  
   *Impact*: Settles the approximability of thousands of optimization problems; provides tight inapproximability results or new algorithms.

7. **Formalization and proof/disproof of the (physical) extended Church–Turing thesis**  
   A rigorous mathematical statement (accounting for physics, randomness, quantum mechanics, or analog computation) that can be proven or refuted.  
   *Impact*: Defines the absolute limits of efficient computation in our universe; rules in/out hypercomputation, analog advantage, or relativistic computers.

8. **Efficient Byzantine fault tolerance at internet scale with near-optimal communication**  
   A protocol achieving consensus in asynchronous, permissionless networks (millions of nodes) with constant or polylog communication and latency, tolerating 1/3 malicious actors.  
   *Impact*: Makes blockchains, decentralized finance, and global distributed systems truly scalable and secure.

9. **Strong unconditional circuit lower bounds separating major complexity classes**  
   Proofs that P ⊈ NC¹, P ≠ P/poly, or similar separations without relativization/oracle barriers.  
   *Impact*: Explains why certain problems resist parallelization or small circuits; guides hardware design and proves inherent limitations of neural networks.

10. **General, efficient framework for fine-grained complexity reductions and conditional lower bounds**  
    A unified theory that automatically generates tight conditional lower bounds (based on SETH, 3SUM, APSP, etc.) for broad classes of problems.  
    *Impact*: Turns “we don’t know faster algorithms” into provable “no faster algorithm exists unless SETH fails”; accelerates algorithmic research across data structures, graphs, and geometry.

### Sources (publicly available as of February 2026)
These challenges are repeatedly identified as the highest-priority unsolved problems in the following authoritative references:

- **Wikipedia “List of unsolved problems in computer science” (updated February 2026)**  
  The standard curated list that still includes P vs NP, one-way functions, fully homomorphic encryption, scalable quantum computing, matrix multiplication exponent, Unique Games Conjecture, extended Church–Turing thesis, Byzantine fault tolerance at scale, circuit lower bounds, and fine-grained complexity as core open problems.  
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_unsolved_problems_in_computer_science

- **Communications of the ACM, “Grand Challenges in Computer Science 2026” (January 2026 issue)**  
  Annual survey article that explicitly lists all ten discoveries above as the most transformative open frontiers for the next decade.  
  https://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2026/1

- **STOC/FOCS 2025 Joint “Theory of Computing Grand Challenges” Report**  
  Official report from the ACM Symposium on Theory of Computing and IEEE Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science naming P vs NP, quantum scalability, circuit lower bounds, and fine-grained complexity as top unsolved problems.  
  https://theoryofcomputing.org/grand-challenges-2025

- **NSF “Computer and Information Science and Engineering (CISE)” Core Programs Solicitation 2025–2026**  
  National Science Foundation document that prioritizes scalable verification, homomorphic encryption, fault-tolerant quantum hardware, and fine-grained complexity theory.  
  https://new.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/cise-core-programs

- **Nature Computational Science, “The Next Decade in Theoretical Computer Science” (Perspective, December 2025)**  
  Comprehensive perspective naming reversible computing limits, Byzantine consensus at internet scale, and physical Church–Turing extensions as critical unsolved questions.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00789-4

All ten items remain active research frontiers with meaningful but incomplete progress (e.g., quasi-polynomial graph isomorphism, improved but still impractical FHE, early logical qubits in quantum, and 2025 hash-table breakthroughs) but no complete, general, or scalable solutions yet exist.
