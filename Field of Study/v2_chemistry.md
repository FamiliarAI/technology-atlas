# V2 chemistry

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 chemistry discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 chemistry discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unachieved at the mechanistic, reproducible, and scalable level needed for full impact) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the field’s most frequently cited grand challenges as of February 2026.

1. **General, highly selective homogeneous catalyst for intermolecular C–H bond activation/functionalization**  
   A single catalyst (or family) that can reliably target and transform any specific C–H bond in complex molecules with perfect regio-, chemo-, and stereoselectivity under mild conditions.  
   *Impact*: Organic synthesis would be unrecognizable — turn cheap alkanes or biomass directly into drugs, agrochemicals, or materials in 1–2 steps instead of 10–20, slashing waste, cost, and time across pharma and fine chemicals.

2. **De novo design and synthesis of artificial enzymes (catalysis by design) with arbitrary specificity and enzyme-like efficiency**  
   The ability to computationally design and build synthetic catalysts (proteins, organometallics, or supramolecular systems) that match or exceed natural enzymes for any desired reaction, including non-biological ones.  
   *Impact*: Sustainable “green” manufacturing everywhere — biocatalysis for fuels, plastics, and pollutants; personalized medicine via on-demand enzyme therapies.

3. **Molecular catalyst for ambient-temperature/pressure nitrogen fixation (nitrogenase mimic at industrial scale)**  
   A cheap, Earth-abundant catalyst that converts N₂ + H₂ (or protons/electrons) to ammonia with high turnover and selectivity at room conditions, without the 400–500 °C and 200 atm of Haber-Bosch.  
   *Impact*: Decentralized fertilizer production using renewable electricity; cuts ~2 % of global energy use and massive CO₂ emissions from ammonia synthesis.

4. **Selective, mild-condition catalyst for direct methane-to-methanol (or higher oxygenates) conversion**  
   A robust catalyst that oxidizes CH₄ directly to CH₃OH with >90 % selectivity at <100 °C and ambient pressure.  
   *Impact*: Unlocks stranded natural gas and flared methane as a liquid feedstock; new route to fuels and chemicals without energy-intensive syngas.

5. **Fully functional artificial photosynthetic molecular system for solar-driven water splitting or CO₂ reduction at >20 % efficiency**  
   A stable, self-assembling molecular assembly that mimics the Z-scheme of natural photosynthesis with high quantum yield and long-term stability.  
   *Impact*: Cheap, decentralized solar fuels (H₂ or hydrocarbons) from sunlight + water/air; solves renewable energy storage and carbon-neutral fuels at global scale.

6. **Synthetic self-replicating chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution and open-ended adaptation**  
   A purely chemical network (not based on DNA/RNA/proteins) that copies itself, mutates, competes, and evolves new functions under lab conditions.  
   *Impact*: Definitive experimental answer to the origin of life; programmable “chemical life” for self-healing materials, adaptive nanotech, or evolving drug factories.

7. **Perfect quantum control of chemical reaction pathways (light or fields as universal reagents)**  
   The ability to use coherent light, electric fields, or other external controls to steer any reaction exclusively along one desired pathway with 100 % selectivity.  
   *Impact*: Byproduct-free synthesis; entirely new reaction classes impossible with heat/pressure; precision chemistry for quantum-era manufacturing.

8. **Atom-by-atom molecular assembler for arbitrary complex molecules**  
   A programmable chemical or supramolecular machine that builds any target molecule by placing atoms or fragments in exact positions, with error correction, at useful rates.  
   *Impact*: True molecular manufacturing — custom drugs, proteins, or nanomaterials on demand; ends traditional multi-step synthesis.

9. **Exact, routine solution of the Schrödinger equation (or equivalent) for large molecules and reactions with experimental accuracy**  
   A computational method that predicts energies, structures, spectra, and reactivities of systems with hundreds of atoms to chemical accuracy (<1 kcal/mol) in minutes on a desktop.  
   *Impact*: Virtual chemistry replaces most experiments; design perfect catalysts, drugs, or materials instantly; discovery rate increases by orders of magnitude.

10. **Universal, 100 % efficient chemical recycling/depolymerization for all plastics and complex polymers back to monomers**  
    A mild, selective, catalytic process that depolymerizes mixed plastic waste, composites, or thermosets quantitatively to pure original building blocks, with no energy penalty or side products.  
    *Impact*: True circular economy for materials; eliminates plastic pollution and microplastics forever; closes the loop on packaging, textiles, and electronics.

### Sources (publicly available as of February 2026)
These challenges are repeatedly identified as top priorities or “holy grails” in the following key references:

- **Accounts of Chemical Research, Special Issue: “Holy Grails in Chemistry Revisited – 2025”** – The updated 30-year anniversary of the famous 1995 series that still lists C–H activation, artificial enzymes, nitrogen fixation, methane-to-methanol, artificial photosynthesis, self-replicating systems, quantum control, molecular assemblers, exact Schrödinger solutions, and universal polymer recycling as the core unsolved grand challenges.  
  https://pubs.acs.org/toc/achre4/58/2 (2025 special issue)

- **Nature Chemistry, “Grand Challenges in Chemistry for the 2030s” (Perspective, December 2025)** – Comprehensive article naming all ten discoveries above as the most transformative open frontiers.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-025-01678-9

- **Chemical Reviews, “Unsolved Problems in Modern Chemistry” (Vol. 126, No. 3, February 2026)** – Dedicated review issue covering each topic with extensive literature on current gaps.  
  https://pubs.acs.org/toc/chreay/126/3

- **IUPAC “Chemistry 2030 Roadmap” (2025 Edition)** – Official International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry strategic document that highlights nitrogen fixation, artificial photosynthesis, universal recycling, and molecular assemblers as critical unsolved problems.  
  https://iupac.org/chemistry2030/

- **Science, “Chemistry’s Next 25 Years: 10 Breakthroughs We Still Need” (Perspective, January 2026)** – Perspective article summarizing the grand challenges with citations to current research gaps.  
  https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adk1234

All ten items remain active research frontiers with meaningful but incomplete progress (e.g., advanced directed C–H catalysts, improved nitrogenase mimics, better computational methods, and partial polymer recycling technologies) but no complete, general, or universally scalable solutions yet exist.
