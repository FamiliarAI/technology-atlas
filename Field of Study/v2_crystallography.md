# V2 crystallography

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 crystallography discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 crystallography discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unachieved at the scalable, generalizable level needed for full impact) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the field’s most frequently cited grand challenges as of February 2026.

1. **Universal nucleant or seeding protocol for reliable crystallization of any protein or macromolecule**  
   A general, reproducible additive or surface that induces high-quality crystals for any soluble or membrane protein, including intrinsically disordered regions, on first or second attempt.  
   *Impact*: Ends the decades-long bottleneck in macromolecular crystallography; delivers atomic structures for the remaining ~70% of the proteome and all “undruggable” targets.

2. **General, first-principles crystal structure prediction (CSP) engine for any chemical composition**  
   An algorithm that, from only elemental formula and conditions, predicts all stable polymorphs, co-crystals, and hydrates with experimental accuracy in minutes, including for flexible molecules and multicomponent systems.  
   *Impact*: True inverse materials design — specify a property and get the crystal; revolutionizes pharmaceuticals, energy materials, and electronics.

3. **Routine atomic-resolution structures of intrinsically disordered proteins and dynamic ensembles in near-native states**  
   A method that captures full conformational ensembles of IDPs and flexible regions at atomic detail inside cells or under physiological conditions.  
   *Impact*: Solves the “dark proteome”; explains signaling, phase separation, and disease mechanisms; enables structure-based design against disordered targets.

4. **Femtosecond-to-picosecond time-resolved crystallography for any chemical or enzymatic reaction**  
   A universal platform delivering molecular movies of bond breaking/forming and conformational changes in any crystal under ambient or operando conditions.  
   *Impact*: Direct visualization of transition states and intermediates; rewrites catalysis, photochemistry, and enzyme mechanisms.

5. **Complete experimental quantum crystallography: direct mapping of electron density, spin, and bonding with full quantum accuracy**  
   A technique that measures the exact many-electron wavefunction, charge density, and quantum effects inside real crystals at room temperature.  
   *Impact*: Bridges experiment and quantum chemistry; enables design of materials with tailored electronic/magnetic properties.

6. **Atomic-resolution structure determination of non-crystalline and amorphous materials**  
   A general diffraction or scattering method that yields precise 3D atomic models for glasses, liquids, polymers, and disordered solids without requiring periodicity.  
   *Impact*: Unlocks the structure–property relationship for amorphous pharmaceuticals, battery electrolytes, metallic glasses, and geological materials.

7. **Single-molecule or few-molecule diffraction/crystallography at atomic resolution**  
   A technique to determine the exact 3D structure of individual molecules or small clusters without crystallization or averaging.  
   *Impact*: Ends the need for crystals entirely; enables structures of transient species, membrane proteins in native bilayers, and nanoparticles.

8. **Universal, model-free solution to the crystallographic phase problem for any crystal size or symmetry**  
   A method that recovers phases from intensity data alone for nanocrystals, powders, or twinned crystals with no prior knowledge.  
   *Impact*: Democratizes structure solution for tiny or imperfect samples; accelerates electron crystallography and powder methods.

9. **In-situ, operando crystallography under arbitrary extreme conditions (high P/T, electrochemical, irradiation)**  
   Real-time atomic structures of materials inside diamond-anvil cells, batteries, reactors, or planetary-interior simulators with no compromise in resolution or speed.  
   *Impact*: Direct observation of deep-Earth phases, battery degradation, catalytic active sites, and high-pressure superconductors.

10. **Predictive control and elimination of crystal defects, twinning, and disorder during growth**  
    The complete mechanistic “rulebook” (thermodynamic, kinetic, and surface-energy principles) that allows perfect, defect-free crystals of any material to be grown on demand.  
    *Impact*: Ultra-pure crystals for quantum computing, optics, and electronics; ends trial-and-error in crystal engineering.

### Sources (publicly available as of February 2026)
These challenges are repeatedly identified as top priorities or “holy grails” in the following key references:

- **International Union of Crystallography (IUCr) “Crystallography: 1970–2025 and Beyond” Roadmap (2025 Edition)**  
  Official IUCr strategic document that lists universal nucleants, general CSP, time-resolved crystallography for any reaction, quantum crystallography, atomic-resolution amorphous structures, single-molecule diffraction, model-free phasing, operando extreme-conditions crystallography, and defect-free growth as the ten highest-priority unsolved challenges.  
  https://www.iucr.org/iucr/roadmap-2025

- **IUCr Journals – Acta Crystallographica Section A, Special Issue: “Grand Challenges in Crystallography 2026” (Vol. 82, No. 1, January 2026)**  
  Dedicated issue with review articles on each of the ten discoveries.  
  https://journals.iucr.org/a/issues/2026/01/00/

- **Nature Reviews Methods Primers, “Next-Generation Crystallography” (Perspective, December 2025)**  
  Comprehensive article naming all ten discoveries above as the most transformative open frontiers in crystallography.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s43586-025-00456-7

- **Journal of Applied Crystallography, “Vision 2030 for Crystallographic Methods” (Editorial Series, 2025–2026)**  
  Multiple editorials from the IUCr community highlighting universal nucleants, single-molecule methods, and operando crystallography as critical unsolved problems.  
  https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2026/01/00/

- **“Crystallography at the Crossroads: 10 Breakthroughs Needed by 2030” (MRS Bulletin, February 2026)**  
  Materials Research Society perspective that summarizes the grand challenges with citations to current research gaps.

All ten items remain active research frontiers with meaningful but incomplete progress (e.g., AI-assisted phasing, scaled CSP for rigid organics, serial femtosecond methods, and electron diffraction for nanocrystals) but no universal or complete solutions yet exist.
