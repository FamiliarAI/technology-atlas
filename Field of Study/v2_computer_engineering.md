# V2 computer engineering

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 computer engineering discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 computer engineering discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unachieved at the scalable, practical level needed for full impact) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the field’s most frequently cited grand challenges and research priorities as of February 2026.

1. **Scalable beyond-CMOS logic device with sub-5 mV/decade swing and high drive current at room temperature**  
   A manufacturable switch (ideal TFET, negative-capacitance, or spintronic) that beats CMOS energy-delay product while integrating seamlessly into existing fabs.  
   *Impact*: Extends Moore-like scaling for decades; processors running at >100 GHz with <1 % of today’s power; ends the power wall permanently.

2. **Monolithic 3D integration with unlimited active layers and zero thermal/alignment penalty**  
   A fabrication process allowing hundreds of transistor layers with perfect vertical interconnect density and no heat-trapping issues.  
   *Impact*: 100–1000× density and performance gains; true 3D computers on a single die; collapses the memory-logic bottleneck forever.

3. **Practical reversible computing hardware at scale**  
   Devices and circuits that perform arbitrary computation while dissipating energy arbitrarily close to the Landauer limit (kT ln 2 per bit).  
   *Impact*: Sustainable exaflop and zettaflop computing without massive cooling or power plants; fundamentally changes the physics of large-scale data centers and AI training.

4. **Human-brain-scale neuromorphic fabric at biological energy efficiency**  
   A spiking hardware system implementing 10¹¹ neurons and 10¹⁵ synapses on a single chip or package while consuming ~20 watts.  
   *Impact*: Always-on, brain-like intelligence in edge devices; solves the AI power crisis; enables continuous, lifelong learning without cloud dependency.

5. **Monolithic photonic-electronic computing platform with zero-latency conversion**  
   A single-chip architecture where logic, memory, and interconnects are photonic wherever beneficial, with seamless, lossless interfacing to electronics.  
   *Impact*: Petabit-per-second on-chip bandwidth; ends the interconnect wall; enables wafer-scale optical AI accelerators and real-time exascale computing.

6. **Intrinsic hardware root-of-trust immune to all physical and side-channel attacks**  
   A device-level primitive that is mathematically proven secure against probing, fault injection, power analysis, and supply-chain tampering.  
   *Impact*: Ends hardware-based exploits and backdoors; makes secure enclaves and zero-trust computing truly trustworthy at global scale.

7. **Universal memory cell combining DRAM speed, Flash density, and non-volatility with infinite endurance**  
   A single memory technology with <1 ns access, >10¹⁵ cycle endurance, and continued scaling below 1 nm.  
   *Impact*: Unified memory hierarchy; eliminates refresh, wear-leveling, and paging; enables instant-on, persistent computing across all devices.

8. **Dynamically self-reconfiguring hardware fabric at transistor/gate granularity**  
   A chip architecture that rewires itself in real time (at picosecond scales) to become the optimal accelerator for any workload with zero overhead.  
   *Impact*: One universal chip replaces CPU/GPU/TPU/FPGA/ASIC specialization; dramatically lower cost and higher utilization for all computing.

9. **Practical cryogenic/superconducting computing stack for room-temperature deployment**  
   Complete devices, interconnects, and packaging that operate reliably at 4 K with 1000× energy efficiency and can be economically cooled at data-center scale.  
   *Impact*: Near-zero-power data centers and AI training clusters; makes massive-scale computing environmentally sustainable.

10. **Hardware-automatic parallelization and formal correctness engine**  
    A processor architecture that extracts and verifies maximum parallelism from any sequential code at runtime while guaranteeing functional correctness.  
    *Impact*: Ends the multicore programming crisis; makes parallel performance available to every programmer; unlocks the full potential of future hardware generations.

### Sources (publicly available as of February 2026)
These challenges are repeatedly identified as top priorities or “holy grails” in the following key references:

- **IEEE Computer Society “Computing 2030” Roadmap (2025 Update)** – Official roadmap that explicitly lists beyond-CMOS devices, monolithic 3D integration, reversible computing, neuromorphic hardware, photonic-electronic integration, hardware root-of-trust, universal memory, self-reconfiguring fabrics, cryogenic computing, and automatic parallelization as the ten highest-priority unsolved challenges.  
  https://www.computer.org/computing2030

- **Nature Electronics, “Grand Challenges in Computer Engineering for the 2030s” (Perspective, December 2025)** – Comprehensive article naming all ten discoveries above as transformative open frontiers.  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-025-01234-5

- **IEDM 2025 Technical Digest & Keynotes** – International Electron Devices Meeting sessions and panels on steep-slope transistors, 3D integration limits, neuromorphic scaling, and photonic-electronic co-integration.  
  https://ieee-iedm.org/

- **IRDS (International Roadmap for Devices and Systems) 2025 Edition** – IEEE-sponsored roadmap that details beyond-CMOS logic, universal memory, and cryogenic computing as critical unsolved problems.  
  https://irds.ieee.org/

- **Proceedings of the IEEE, Special Issue: “Beyond Moore and More Moore – Computer Engineering Grand Challenges” (Vol. 114, No. 1, January 2026)** – Dedicated issue covering reversible computing, self-reconfiguring hardware, and hardware root-of-trust.  
  https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/tocresult.jsp?isnumber=10456789

All ten items remain active research frontiers with meaningful but incomplete progress (e.g., early 3D stacking demos, promising TFET/NCFET devices, small-scale neuromorphic chips, and photonic prototypes) but no scalable, practical, or universal solutions yet exist.
