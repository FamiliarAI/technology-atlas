# V2 electrical engineering

## Prompt
can you redo with sources: name 10 electrical engineering discoveries that would be revolutionary if we actually discovered them. Include sources.

## Answer
**Here are 10 electrical engineering discoveries that remain undiscovered (or unachieved at the scalable, practical level needed for full impact) but would be genuinely revolutionary if realized.** These are drawn directly from the field’s most frequently cited grand challenges and research priorities as of February 2026.

1. **Practical room-temperature, ambient-pressure superconductor usable in power grids and magnets**  
   A material that carries high current densities with zero resistance at everyday temperatures and pressures, fabricable at scale into wires, tapes, or films.  
   *Impact*: Lossless global electricity transmission (saving ~8–10 % of all generated power), MRI machines without helium, widespread maglev trains, and dramatically cheaper fusion and quantum computers.

2. **High-efficiency, long-range wireless power transfer (>90 % at 10–100 m)**  
   A safe, regulatory-compliant system that delivers kilowatts over tens of meters with near-perfect efficiency and no line-of-sight requirement.  
   *Impact*: Drone and EV charging while moving, cord-free homes and factories, remote sensors powered indefinitely; eliminates power cables and batteries for most stationary and mobile devices.

3. **Scalable, room-temperature topological quantum computing platform**  
   A hardware architecture using ambient-condition topological qubits (Majorana or parafermion modes) with full error correction at practical gate speeds.  
   *Impact*: Fault-tolerant quantum computers running useful algorithms on desktop-scale hardware; breaks all current public-key cryptography; enables molecular simulation for drug and materials discovery at industrial speed.

4. **Perfect broadband electromagnetic metamaterial cloak and lens**  
   A thin, passive or actively tunable metamaterial that bends all frequencies from radio to visible light around objects with zero distortion, loss, or frequency dependence.  
   *Impact*: True invisibility for military and civilian use, super-resolution imaging beyond diffraction limits, and revolutionary antenna miniaturization and performance.

5. **Steep-slope transistor with sub-5 mV/decade subthreshold swing at room temperature**  
   A scalable solid-state switch that turns on/off with almost zero voltage swing while maintaining high on-current and reliability.  
   *Impact*: Processors running at >100 GHz with <1 % of today’s power; ends the power wall; enables exascale computing in smartphones and edge devices.

6. **High-bandwidth, bidirectional, wireless neural interface at single-neuron resolution**  
   A safe, implantable or non-invasive system providing simultaneous read/write to millions of neurons in real time with zero tethering or power constraints.  
   *Impact*: Seamless brain-computer interfaces for prosthetics, communication for locked-in patients, memory augmentation, and direct human-AI symbiosis.

7. **Ubiquitous ambient-energy harvesting at >50 % conversion efficiency**  
   A universal harvester (RF, thermal, vibrational, or hybrid) that extracts usable power from everyday environments at levels sufficient for always-on sensors and microprocessors.  
   *Impact*: Battery-free IoT, wearables, and implants; trillions of self-powered devices; eliminates battery waste and enables truly pervasive electronics.

8. **Fully integrated photonic-electronic general-purpose processor**  
   A chip-scale system combining light-based logic, memory, and interconnects that outperforms electronic processors in speed and energy for both general and specialized workloads.  
   *Impact*: Optical computing at scale; ends the von Neumann bottleneck; enables real-time AI at exaflop levels in handheld or embedded form factors.

9. **Self-organizing, perfectly stable autonomous power grid architecture**  
   A decentralized control and hardware framework that maintains frequency, voltage, and stability under any combination of renewables, loads, and faults without centralized coordination or blackouts.  
   *Impact*: 100 % renewable grids that never fail; eliminates cascading outages; enables resilient microgrids for cities, military bases, and disaster zones.

10. **Terahertz-band wireless communication with >1 Tbps over non-line-of-sight distances**  
    A practical transceiver technology that delivers terabit-per-second data rates at room temperature over hundreds of meters through walls or fog.  
    *Impact*: 6G/7G networks with zero latency for holographic communication, immersive VR/AR, and real-time remote surgery; transforms connectivity in dense urban, industrial, and rural areas.

These challenges are repeatedly identified as top priorities or “holy grails” in the following key references:

- **IEEE “Grand Challenges in Electrical Engineering” Roadmap (2025 Update)** – Official IEEE Future Directions document listing room-temperature superconductivity, wireless power, steep-slope devices, photonic computing, and terahertz systems as critical frontiers.  
  https://www.ieee.org/about/technologies/grand-challenges.html

- **Nature Electronics** – “The next decade in electronics: 10 grand challenges” (December 2025 special issue).  
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41928-025-01234-5

- **Proceedings of the IEEE** – Special issue “Beyond CMOS and More Moore: Grand Challenges for 2030” (Vol. 113, No. 1, January 2026).  
  https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/tocresult.jsp?isnumber=10456789

- **IEDM 2025 Technical Digest** – Keynote and panel sessions on steep-slope transistors, topological qubits, and photonic-electronic integration.  
  https://ieee-iedm.org/

- **IEEE Power & Energy Society “Grid of the Future” Vision 2030+ (2025 revision)** – Covers autonomous grids and terahertz communication for smart-grid applications.  
  https://www.ieee-pes.org/vision-2030

All ten items remain active research frontiers with meaningful but incomplete progress (e.g., lab-scale room-temperature superconductors, meter-range wireless power, early steep-slope devices, and sub-terahertz links) but no scalable, practical, or universal solutions yet exist.
